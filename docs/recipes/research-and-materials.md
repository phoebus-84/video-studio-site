# Gather a world, not a folder

**Goal:** a useful body of research and material from which a channel or series can make several different films.

You do not have to arrive with a finished script. Bring a question, a collection, a reading list, footage you have shot, or simply an area you want to explore. The agent can help turn it into proposals you can actually judge.

The important distinction is between **research that supports a claim**, **references that inspire a treatment**, and **assets you can put in a film**. One item can play several roles, but permission for one role does not imply permission for the others.

## Ingredients

- A topic, provisional channel brief or episode question.
- An agent with access to the material you choose to share.
- Any existing sources: books, links, notes, recordings, images or local files.
- A target audience and intended publication context.
- A limit on research scope, time and optional acquisition costs.

**You leave with:** a source shortlist, a research note, annotated visual directions, a rights/provenance record and a list of missing material. The pack may be revised before anything is generated.

**Costs:** agent/research work; paid licences, commissions or generation only if you choose them. Free-to-download does not mean free to redistribute.

## 1. Ask a question the research can answer

A request to “research the nineteenth century” has no useful stopping point. For a poem, you might ask which details in everyday village life help a reader understand the anticipation in the text. For a software film, ask which concrete task the tool changes for its user.

```text
Help me research a visual treatment for a poetry series.
I will provide the source text and a few references I like.
Separate verified facts, interpretations and open questions.
Propose a small selection of period images and visual approaches.
For each, say what it would help the viewer understand and whether we can
actually use it in a video. Do not buy or generate material yet.
```

**The agent brings:** findings with sources, conflicting accounts where relevant, a shortlist and remaining questions.

**You decide:** which interpretation or argument the film will pursue. The most abundant search result is not necessarily the right creative direction.

## 2. Contribute what only you have

An operator’s material can be the difference between a generic video and a distinctive one. Add the workshop recording, the annotated page, the unusual object, the pronunciation you know, or the memory that changes the story.

Give context as well as files:

> “These recordings are mine. Use the workshop sound, but not the conversation in the background. The second photograph shows the finished piece; the first is an earlier prototype.”

Ask the agent to report unusable or uncertain items rather than silently fixing everything. You might decide to reshoot a ten-second close-up instead of trying to rescue a blurry frame.

Sensitive material should not be sent to a cloud provider merely because it is in the source folder. Agree on what can leave your machine.

## 3. Review a proposal, not a pile of downloads

A useful proposal pairs a sample with a reason.

| Candidate | What the agent should explain | What you choose |
|---|---|---|
| Factual source | Authority, date, relevant claim and uncertainty | Whether the evidence is sufficient |
| Visual reference | Composition, light, texture or rhythm worth learning from | The direction, not a licence to copy it |
| Production image/clip | Intended shot, resolution, crop options, source and rights | Whether to acquire or include it |
| Sound or voice | Dramatic role, listening sample, permissions and editing constraints | Whether it belongs in the film |
| Missing material | Why existing sources cannot do the job | Record, commission, generate, simplify or omit |

Ask to see a small contact sheet with stable labels, not dozens of near-duplicates. Give feedback by label: “Keep B for the light, reject D because it romanticises the setting, and find a wider view of C.”

An image that is historically authentic can still mislead when placed beside the wrong claim. Check meaning as well as appearance.

## 4. Resolve rights before production depends on the asset

For acquired material, retain the original source URL, creator, licence, evidence and required credit. Check whether modifications or redistribution have conditions. A public-domain poem does not automatically make a modern translation, reading or museum photograph public domain.

The studio’s archive tools can preserve licence/provenance records for supported sources. For your own material or a commission, keep the permission evidence with the asset. If the status is unclear, leave it unresolved; do not turn “probably fine” into an approval.

If you choose generation, agree on the provider, references that may be uploaded, the small test to make and a spending limit. Review the test before commissioning the whole sequence.

## 5. Separate shared sources from episode choices

In a poetry channel, the author’s room can be shared by many poems. One poem’s verified text, accepted recital, timing and paintings can be shared by its vertical and horizontal films. The two films still need separate editorial treatments.

Ask the agent to distinguish:

- **Channel or author material:** used across several series or episodes.
- **Work-specific material:** the source pack for this poem, object or subject.
- **Episode decisions:** which sources appear, how they are framed and what the narration argues.

The studio supports shared, pinned sources. The channel’s procedure must define what the pack contains and how to check that it is ready. A folder’s existence is not proof that its text, images and audio are complete.

## 6. Leave an actionable handoff

Before production, ask for a short review:

```text
Show the selected sources and their intended uses.
List missing material, unresolved rights and claims that still need checking.
Distinguish reusable channel material from this episode's choices.
Propose the smallest test we should make next, with its cost if any.
Save my choices with the project; do not leave them only in this conversation.
```

**Ready when:** the next person can find the material, understand why it was selected, see what is permitted, and know what still needs a decision. They should not need to repeat the entire search.
