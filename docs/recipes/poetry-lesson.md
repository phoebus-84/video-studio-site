# One poem. Two films.

**Goal:** ask for a new poem and receive two deliberately different works: a 9:16 recital and a complete 16:9 lesson, built on the same researched material.

For a prepared *Poesie al museo* channel, the operator’s request can be this short:

```text
fai il sabato del villaggio, sia la poesia 9/16 sia la lezione completa
```

In English: “Make *Il sabato del villaggio*, both the 9:16 poem and the complete lesson.”

The short request is the **result of preparation**, not a replacement for it. The channel has already established its museum world, roles, visual standards, voice choices, series formats and production procedure. The agent can use that saved context instead of asking the operator to design the whole channel again.

This is the operator’s example request, not a claim that these particular Leopardi videos are already in the gallery. The [Sonnet 18 example](../examples#sonnet) shows a real vertical production from the channel’s English-language work. The channel itself is not bundled with a fresh studio installation.

## Ingredients

- A prepared poetry channel, or time to [develop one](./build-a-channel).
- Two agreed products: a vertical recital and a horizontal lesson.
- An accurate text with a documented source and permission to reproduce it.
- An agent, working media/render tools and an agreed spending/review policy.
- Narration sources and visual material appropriate to the language and work.

**You leave with:** a shared poem source pack, two episode treatments, two timelines and two reviewed video candidates—not one edit exported twice.

**Costs:** shared research and source production, any format-specific additions, and two compositions/renders. Reuse avoids making the same material twice; it does not remove the cost of thinking through two films.

## Before the short prompt: build the channel

The real channel is organised as a museum: each author has a room. A room holds its atmosphere and reusable author material; a poem has its own text, recital, timing, pictures and sounds. Different series use those sources for different purposes.

That structure came from choices, tests and operator feedback.

| Earlier decision | What you and the agent prepare | What later episodes can reuse |
|---|---|---|
| Who is this for? | Audience, teaching approach, tone and source standards | A consistent editorial purpose |
| What is the world? | Museum concept, room studies, light, type and palette proposals | A recognisable visual family |
| Who guides and who reads? | Character references, role separation, voice auditions and permissions | Recurring identities without a new casting exercise |
| What is a recital? | A vertical pilot with verse, performance and visual rhythm | Its layout and production rules |
| What is a lesson? | A horizontal pilot with explanation, examples and room to read | A different structure and set of checks |
| How are corrections remembered? | Channel/series instructions, accepted examples and versioned formats | Decisions that survive a new conversation |

**The human work matters.** You choose the concept, contribute references, compare character and room proposals, listen to voices and decide what the pilots get right. The agent researches and prepares alternatives, makes the selected tests, implements the agreed format and saves the decisions.

[Develop the channel](./build-a-channel), [prepare the cast](./create-a-character), then [prove the two series](./repeatable-series). This can take several sessions. There is no honest universal “five-minute setup” for a distinctive channel.

## A new author is not just another filename

An existing room can be reused. A new author may need a room treatment before episode production: palette and period references, an author portrait treatment, character/voice choices, sample opening and closing frames, and proposed teaching material.

In *Poesie al museo*, that preparatory package is itself something the operator can review. It lets a person reject the room, the casting or the direction before paying for complete films.

For an unprepared author, ask the agent to show what is missing and which decisions it needs. Keep the request’s scope explicit: asking for the two poem videos is not permission to produce every other format or a biography as an extra.

## 1. Resolve the request against the saved context

When the channel is established, the agent should first identify:

- the requested poem and language;
- the author’s room, and whether it is ready;
- the two series and the exact releases in use;
- existing sources, takes and accepted material worth reusing;
- missing research or media, expected costs and required checkpoints.

In a fresh session, name the channel and ask it to read the channel’s entry instructions before giving the short request. The brevity relies on accessible files, not invisible memory.

A useful response is: “The room is ready. I’ll prepare the poem’s source once, then make the recital and lesson using their existing formats. Here is the material still missing and the review I need.” If the room is not ready, it should say that instead.

## 2. Research and prepare the poem once

Verify the edition, complete text, punctuation and line breaks. Keep the author’s words distinct from teaching commentary. Public-domain status of a poem does not automatically cover a modern translation, recording or illustration.

For *Il sabato del villaggio*, a possible teaching question is: **why can anticipation feel richer than the celebration itself?** That is a proposed direction to research and choose, not a substitute for textual evidence.

Ask for a small source proposal:

```text
For the two requested films, prepare one shared source plan.
Verify the full poem and its source. Suggest a teaching question for the lesson,
a recital performance direction, and visual material tied to particular lines.
Show which material already exists and what must be made or acquired.
Keep facts, quotations and interpretation distinct. Let me review unresolved
editorial choices before paid production.
```

**You contribute:** a preferred edition, an interpretation, a recording, images or corrections to the proposed reading. **The agent contributes:** research, visual proposals, provenance checks and a plan for the missing material.

## 3. Make the accepted reading and shared assets

Audition the poem’s voice if the existing direction does not settle the performance. Check difficult words, enjambment, pace and pauses. Listen to the whole reading—not only a few technically clean segments.

Retain the selected take and align it to the written text. That measured timing lets both formats relate their visual emphasis to the same performance. A source correction becomes a new source version; it should not silently alter an already delivered film.

Choose pictures because they illuminate a line or an idea. Review the candidate sheet before animating it. Reuse suitable room assets and existing motion rather than regenerating them for each output.

**Duration needs judgement.** A vertical film is not automatically a sixty-second clip. If the complete poem exceeds a format’s intended duration, resolve that openly: agree a longer treatment or a format change. Do not silently omit verses or accelerate the reading to fit.

## 4. Write two treatments, not one crop

| | Vertical recital · 9:16 | Complete lesson · 16:9 |
|---|---|---|
| Main experience | Enter and hear the poem | Understand something about its language, context and effect |
| Voice | The accepted recital, with its performance intact | The guide’s explanation, with readings or quotations as the format requires |
| Images | Concentrated visual rhythm around the verse | Evidence, comparisons, details and visual demonstrations |
| Text | Verse that remains readable on a phone | Quotations and teaching graphics with room to breathe |
| Structure | The recital format’s opening, reading and closing | A question, a developing argument, examples and a conclusion |
| Shared material | Poem text, source take/timing, relevant pictures and room assets | The same source pack, selected and composed for another purpose |

The lesson needs its own script and generally additional narration. It should not simply repeat the poem on screen for several minutes. The vertical film should not be a dense lesson squeezed into a narrow frame.

Ask to see the two outlines side by side before building them. The shared source is a foundation; it is not a requirement to repeat every shot or phrase.

## 5. Review each film on its own terms

For the **recital**, listen for missing words and unnatural joins, read long lines at phone size, and check that the pictures serve the spoken moment.

For the **lesson**, ask whether a newcomer can follow the argument on first hearing. Check factual claims, the relationship between quotations and commentary, the time available to inspect a detail, and whether a spoken invitation such as “look” is actually supported on screen.

Use layout sheets before full renders, then watch and listen to the candidates. The channel’s agreed policy determines which steps need a human stop and which can use recorded automated review. An earlier decision can delegate a routine check; it is not permission for the agent to invent a human approval.

A useful correction is scoped: “Keep the recital. In the lesson, introduce the final question earlier and give the village detail time to be seen.” The agent should identify what changes, preserve the earlier versions and avoid regenerating the shared reading unnecessarily.

## 6. Deliver a pair that another person can understand

Keep the shared source and its provenance, the selected release for each series, the two scripts/specs and timelines, captions, technical reports and clear output paths. State which candidates have been reviewed and what remains open.

Your next request can now be short because the durable work is in the channel: the production method, the cast, the standards and the material worth reusing. The new poem still deserves research and direction.

That is the advantage: **less repeated setup, not less authorship.**

## Acceptance check

- Both requested products exist and have the intended aspect ratios.
- The text and reading are complete or any intentional adaptation was explicitly agreed.
- The lesson teaches; the recital gives the poem space.
- Shared sources are reused intentionally, with no unrequested changes to earlier films.
- New decisions and corrections are saved at channel, series or episode level.
- The operator can judge the two films independently.

This case study is based on the channel’s documented room/poem/series organisation, paired-request procedure and review practices. Its custom skills, voices, assets and formats remain library-specific; the reusable lesson is how to prepare your own channel.
